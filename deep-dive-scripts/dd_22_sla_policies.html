1
What SLA Policies Do

SLA Policies let you define formal response and resolution time commitments in Rev.io PSA and have the system track them automatically on every ticket. Once a policy is configured and assigned to a customer, every ticket for that customer displays real-time SLA timers — color-coded to show whether you're on track, escalating, or breached. Your team never has to calculate response times manually or hunt through ticket timestamps to know if they're within SLA.

SLA policies are configured in System Settings and assigned to customer accounts. The system applies the policy automatically when a ticket is created for that customer.

→ Connects ToSLA Policies connect to: Customers (assigned at the account level as the default policy), Tickets (timers display on every ticket, visible in the ticket list), System Settings (business hours and holidays are factored into timer calculations), Workflows (workflow notifications can trigger on SLA escalation or breach thresholds), and Analytics (SLA performance reports).
▲ Platform ImpactWithout SLA policies, your team has no systemic way to know which tickets are at risk of breaching a commitment. SLA management becomes reactive — someone has to remember to check, and things slip. With policies configured, the system surfaces risk automatically so your team can prioritize before a breach happens, not after.
2
Creating an SLA Policy — Step by Step

Navigate to Admin > System Settings > SLA Policies > Create/Edit SLA Policies. The policy wizard walks you through several configuration steps.

Policy Name and Description

Give the policy a name that identifies which customers or agreements it applies to. Examples: 'Standard Support SLA,' 'Enterprise Priority SLA,' 'Platinum Managed Service SLA.'

Response and Resolution Targets by Priority

For each ticket priority level in your system, define two targets:

TargetWhat It MeasuresExample
Response TimeTime from ticket creation to first meaningful response (first public activity note or status change from New)Priority Critical: 1 hour. Priority High: 4 hours. Priority Medium: 8 hours. Priority Low: Next business day.
Resolution TimeTime from ticket creation to ticket closurePriority Critical: 4 hours. Priority High: 1 business day. Priority Medium: 3 business days. Priority Low: 5 business days.
❓ PromptLet's go through each priority level. For each one: how quickly do you commit to responding, and how quickly do you commit to resolving? Use your current SLA agreements or standard of care as the reference.

Timer Configuration — Start, Pause, and Stop Conditions

This is where the SLA policy gets precise about what counts as SLA time and what doesn't.

SettingDescriptionCommon Configuration
Timer StartWhat event starts the SLA clockTicket creation (most common) or first assignment to a technician
Timer PauseWhat condition pauses the clockTicket status changes to 'Pending Customer' or 'Waiting on Parts' — time spent waiting on the customer or a third party doesn't count
Timer ResumeWhat resumes the clock after pausingStatus changes back to an active status like 'In Progress'
Timer StopWhat stops the clock permanentlyTicket is closed or resolved
Business Hours OnlyWhether SLA time counts only during configured business hoursMost clients: Yes. 24/7 operations: No.
💡 Pro TipThe Pause condition is critical for fairness. If a ticket is waiting on the customer to respond and the SLA clock keeps running, your team gets penalized for the customer's delay. Configure a 'Pending Customer' or equivalent status as a pause condition.
3
SLA Timer Display on Tickets

Once a policy is active on a customer's account, every ticket for that customer shows SLA timers directly on the ticket detail page and in the ticket list view.

Timer ColorMeaning
⚫ BlackTimer has not started yet — ticket hasn't triggered the start condition
🟢 GreenOn track — well within the SLA target time
🟠 OrangeEscalated — approaching the SLA threshold, action needed soon
🔵 BlueCompleted — SLA target was met, ticket was resolved in time
🔴 RedBreached — SLA target was missed

Both the Response timer and the Resolution timer display independently, each with their own color-coded status. A tech can be on track for response but breaching resolution if the job is taking longer than expected.

💡 Pro TipAdd the SLA Response Time and SLA Resolution Time columns to your default ticket list view. This gives dispatchers and managers SLA status for every open ticket at a glance without opening each one individually.
4
Assigning SLA Policies to Customers

Once policies are built, assign them to customer accounts. Navigate to the customer profile and look for the Default SLA Policy field in the customer's billing or service information. Select the appropriate policy. Every ticket created for this customer from this point forward will use that policy.

❓ PromptDo you have different SLA tiers for different customer types — for example, enterprise customers on a premium SLA vs. standard customers on a basic SLA? Let's map your customer segments to policies.
Customer SegmentRecommended Policy Assignment
Managed Service / EnterpriseStrictest policy — short response and resolution times, business hours only
Standard SupportModerate policy — same-day response, multi-day resolution
Break/Fix / T&MLoose policy or no policy — best effort, no formal commitment
Internal / Test AccountsNo policy assigned
⚠ Watch OutIf a customer doesn't have an SLA policy assigned, no timers appear on their tickets. This is fine for customers with no formal SLA commitment — but verify intentionally. Don't leave managed service customers without a policy by accident.
5
SLA Workflow Notifications

Workflows can trigger automatically based on SLA events. Navigate to Admin > Workflows and create a new workflow with an SLA-related trigger.

SLA Workflow Trigger Options

  • SLA Escalation Threshold Reached: Trigger a notification when a ticket approaches its SLA target — for example, when 80% of the response window has elapsed. Gives your team a warning before the breach.
  • SLA Target Breached: Trigger a notification when a ticket has missed its SLA target. Alert the tech, their manager, and optionally the customer.

Recommended SLA Workflows

WorkflowTriggerRecipientsMessage
SLA WarningEscalation threshold (e.g., 75%)Assigned tech + tech's managerTicket #[ID] is approaching SLA breach for [customer]. Response due in [X hours].
SLA Breach — InternalSLA breachedTech + manager + service managerTicket #[ID] has breached SLA for [customer]. Immediate attention required.

❓ PromptDo you want proactive SLA warnings before a breach, reactive alerts when a breach happens, or both? And should customers be notified automatically when an SLA is breached?
6
SLA Reporting

SLA performance data is available in Analytics. Key reports to monitor:

  • SLA Performance by Technician: Which techs are consistently meeting SLA vs. struggling. Useful for coaching conversations.
  • SLA Performance by Customer: Which customer accounts are generating the most SLA risk. May indicate understaffing or misaligned SLA commitments.
  • SLA Performance by Ticket Type: Are certain types of work (Break/Fix vs. Maintenance vs. Installation) harder to resolve within SLA? Useful for pricing and staffing decisions.
  • Breach Rate Over Time: Is your SLA compliance improving, staying flat, or getting worse month over month?
💡 Pro TipReview SLA performance monthly in your management meeting. Consistent SLA breaches on a specific customer account are a signal that either the SLA targets are too aggressive for your staffing, or that account needs more dedicated resources.

Action Items

  • Define your SLA tiers — how many distinct policy levels do you need?
  • Build one SLA policy per tier
  • Configure pause conditions for 'waiting on customer' and 'waiting on parts' statuses
  • Assign the correct policy to every managed service and contract customer
  • Add SLA timer columns to your default ticket list view
  • Build at least one SLA escalation workflow and one breach workflow
  • Schedule a monthly SLA performance review in Analytics
