1
Why System Settings Matter

System Settings is the control panel for how Rev.io PSA behaves across every module. The decisions we make here affect how tickets are created, how your team is notified, how SLAs are calculated, how time is tracked, and how billing is triggered. Getting this right during onboarding means the platform works your way from day one instead of fighting you.

We'll move through each section together. I'll explain what the setting does, ask for your preference, and we'll configure it in real time. Some of these we can finalize today — others you may need to confirm internally and come back to.

→ Connects ToSystem Settings touches every module in the platform. Ticket defaults drive the Service module. Business hours and holidays affect SLA timers and the Dispatch Board. SMS settings control notifications in Ticketing and Billing. Time clock reasons appear on every ticket timesheet.
💡 Internal NoteNew Categories to Be Aware OfTwo new System Settings categories were added in recent platform updates:

Mobile Options — Controls GPS tracking, geofencing, and automatic status transitions for field technicians using the Rev.io mobile app. Includes default time log reasons for auto-populated travel logs, and automatic ticket status transitions when techs enter/exit a customer location. If the client uses field techs with the mobile app, flag this for configuration.

SLA Policies — A new dedicated category for building formal SLA commitments. Covered in depth in the SLA Policies deep-dive module. If the client has contractual SLA commitments, flag this as a follow-up configuration session after onboarding calls are complete.
2
Company Settings

Start with Company Name and Address. This information is pulled onto every customer-facing document — invoices, quotes, work orders, proposals. It needs to be exactly what you want customers to see.

❓ PromptIs the company name and address correct as shown? Is this your billing address or your primary office address?

Next, Business Hours. These define when your company is officially open and are used to calculate SLA response times and to display availability on the Dispatch Board. If a ticket comes in at 11 PM and your hours are 8 AM–5 PM, the SLA clock doesn't start until 8 AM the next morning.

❓ PromptWhat are your standard business hours, and are they the same Monday through Friday?

Company Holidays work the same way — dates you add here are excluded from SLA calculations. If you don't add them and a ticket comes in on Christmas, the system treats it like any other business day.

❓ PromptDo you have your holiday list for the year? We can add them now or as a follow-up.
▲ Platform ImpactIncorrect business hours or missing holidays will cause your SLA timers to miscalculate — which can make your team look like they're missing response targets when they're actually working within their actual hours.
3
SMS Notifications

Rev.io PSA can send SMS notifications to your internal team — things like ticket assignments and status updates going straight to a tech's phone — and to your customers, so they get text updates when their ticket changes status.

These are separate settings, and you can control whether customers are auto-opted in or have to opt in manually. If a customer wants off the list, they can reply STOP to any PSA text message and the system will automatically opt them out.

❓ PromptDo you want SMS on for your internal team? For customers? And if yes, should customers be auto-opted in to receive texts, or should they have to opt in manually?
💡 Internal NoteThe Customer SMS Default setting controls whether new contacts are automatically opted in when created. Remind the client to check their compliance requirements before enabling auto-opt-in — this is especially important in regulated industries. Customers can always reply STOP to opt out automatically.
4
System Tables — Ticket Settings

Before we configure any defaults, we need to make sure the dropdown options those defaults draw from actually match your business. In Rev.io PSA, System Tables are where you manage all the picklist values — every dropdown menu in the platform pulls from here. Think of this as building the vocabulary the system uses.

Under the Ticket tab in System Tables, we have five areas to review. We'll come back to other System Tables tabs — like Products, Billing, and so on — as those topics come up later in your onboarding calls. For now, let's focus on tickets.

💡 Internal NoteNavigate to Admin > System Settings > System Tables and select the Ticket tab. Walk through each category below. The goal is to make sure the options reflect how this client actually talks about and categorizes their work — not just the platform defaults.

Ticket Status

Ticket statuses represent the stages a ticket moves through from creation to resolution. The platform comes with a default set, but you can add, rename, or reorder them to match your actual workflow. Every status you configure here will appear as an option on tickets and in queue views.

❓ PromptWalk me through the life of a ticket in your business — what stages does it go through? For example, does it start as New, then move to In Progress, then Pending Customer, then Resolved? Let's make sure the statuses in here match that flow.

Ticket Priority

Priority levels help your team triage and sort incoming work. You might use Low, Medium, High, and Critical — or you might have a completely different naming convention. Whatever you use, it needs to live here before we can set a default priority for new tickets.

❓ PromptWhat priority levels do you use? And do they align with anything in your client contracts or SLAs — for example, does a Critical priority trigger a different response time commitment?

Ticket Type

Ticket types categorize the kind of work being done. Common examples are Break/Fix, Maintenance, Installation, or Project. These show up on tickets, in reports, and on invoices if you're billing by work type.

❓ PromptWhat types of work does your team handle? Let's make sure those are represented here. And which one is the most common — that will become your default ticket type.

Severity

Severity is separate from priority — priority is about urgency, severity is about the impact of the issue. A low-priority ticket could still have high severity if it's affecting a large number of users. Not all clients use both, but if you have SLA tiers or enterprise customers, it's worth having.

❓ PromptDo you distinguish between how urgent a ticket is and how much impact it has? If you have formal SLAs or tiered support contracts, severity is worth configuring now.

System Tags

System Tags are a free-form labeling layer that can be applied to tickets for filtering, grouping, and reporting purposes. They work alongside ticket type and status — think of them as additional search terms your team can apply without changing the ticket's actual status or type.

❓ PromptDoes your team use any labels or categories today that don't fit neatly into ticket type? For example, tags like "VIP Customer," "Under Warranty," or "Recurring Issue" — anything like that would live here as a system tag.
→ Connects ToThese System Table values feed directly into the Ticket Defaults we'll configure next, as well as ticket queue views, SLA policies, reporting filters, and invoices. Getting these right now means the defaults and downstream configurations will actually make sense to your team.
5
Services — Ticket Defaults

Now that your ticket statuses, types, and priorities are set up in System Tables, we can configure the defaults — the values the system pre-selects every time a new ticket is created. This is what makes your configuration feel intentional instead of generic.

  • New Ticket Status — the status assigned the moment a ticket is created. Based on what we just configured, which status should a brand new, untouched ticket start in?
  • Assumed Ticket Type — the placeholder type for tickets that haven't been triaged yet. Think of it as 'Unknown' or 'Unclassified' — it exists so nothing is left typeless while it's waiting to be categorized.
  • Default Ticket Type — what gets pre-selected when a tech creates a ticket manually. This should be whichever type we identified as your most common work type.
  • Default Ticket Priority — the starting priority for new tickets before someone reviews and adjusts it. Usually your lowest defined priority, but depends on your workflow.
  • Default Ticket User Group — which user group should new tickets land with first? This is where the user groups created during User Management setup come in.
❓ PromptLet's go through each of these now. I'll read them off and we'll select from the values we just configured in System Tables.

Time Clock Reasons

Also in the Services section — Time Clock Reasons are the categories your team selects when logging time on a ticket. These appear on every ticket timesheet and flow directly into billing and payroll reporting. Common examples are On-Site Labor, Remote Support, Travel Time, and Project Management.

❓ PromptWhat categories do you want your team to choose from when they log time? Let's build that list now — we can always add more later, but a solid starting set helps from day one.
▲ Platform ImpactTime clock reasons are especially important if you bill time to customers — they appear on invoices and determine how hours are categorized in reports. Vague or missing reasons make it harder to support billing disputes and payroll reconciliation.
→ Connects ToTicket defaults directly shape the Service module. Every ticket created — whether by a tech, via email-to-ticket, or through the customer portal — will start with these settings. They also affect how tickets appear in queue views and reporting.
6
Remaining Service Settings

A few more important decisions to round out the Service configuration in System Settings:

  • Email out public activity — when a tech adds a public note to a ticket, should the customer automatically get an email with that update? This is how you keep customers informed without techs having to remember to send separate emails.
  • Signature acceptance clause — the legal language customers agree to when signing off on completed work. Review the default wording — is it acceptable or do you want changes?
  • Ticket tags — do you want your team to be able to add searchable labels to tickets? Useful for filtering and categorizing work outside of ticket type.
❓ PromptLet's go through these one at a time. I'll ask, you answer, and we'll configure as we go.

Action Items

  • Add company holidays if not completed today
  • Confirm signature clause wording with legal if needed
  • Build out your full time entry reasons list if not completed today
7
Inventory — Will You Use It?

Before we move on, we need to make a decision about inventory. Rev.io PSA includes an inventory management module, but it's something you need to consciously opt into — and it affects how the rest of the platform handles parts, products, and costs. So this is a good time to lock that in.

❓ PromptDo you track physical inventory — parts, equipment, materials — that your techs use on jobs? Or do you order parts as needed and don't maintain a stocked warehouse?

If you are using inventory, there's one more decision we need to make right now: your valuation method. This determines how the system calculates the cost of items when they're consumed.

  • FIFO (First In, First Out) — the cost of the oldest stock on hand is applied when an item is used. Common in industries where older inventory should be consumed first.
  • Average Cost — the system maintains a running average of what you paid for each item, and applies that average when items are consumed. Simpler and more commonly used in MSP and field service environments.
❓ PromptIf you're using inventory — do you know which valuation method your accounting team prefers, FIFO or Average Cost?
▲ Platform ImpactThis is not something you can change after you start receiving and consuming inventory. If there's any uncertainty, involve the client's accounting team before making this decision. Choosing the wrong method creates reconciliation problems down the road.
💡 Internal NoteIf the client is not using inventory today but thinks they might in the future, note it and make sure the Inventory Management deep-dive module gets added to their follow-up plan. The setting can be enabled later, but the valuation method decision will need to happen before they receive any stock.
8
Email Inboxes & Outboxes

Rev.io PSA handles two directions of email — inbound and outbound — and they're configured separately. Getting these set up correctly means your team's notifications go out from the right address, and customer emails coming in get routed to the right place automatically.

Inbound Email Options

Under Inbound Email Options, there's one key setting to configure before we get into inbox setup: auto-assign by email domain. If a ticket comes in from someone@acmecorp.com and you have Acme Corp as a customer record, PSA can automatically link that ticket to the right account — no manual lookup needed.

❓ PromptWould you like tickets to automatically match to a customer account based on the sender's email domain? This works best when your customers use consistent company email domains.

Support Inboxes (Inbound)

You can configure multiple support inboxes in the system, and each one can route to a different ticket type or user group. So if you have a support@ address and a billing@ address, those can each feed a different queue automatically — no manual sorting required.

❓ PromptHow many support email addresses do you have that you'd want to receive tickets from? For example, do you have separate inboxes for technical support versus billing or sales?
💡 Internal NoteTo connect an inbox, the client will need to either set up a forwarding rule from their mail server to the PSA-provided address, or provide IMAP credentials depending on the integration method. Confirm this with the client before or after the call and flag it as a technical setup task if needed.

Spam Filters

There's also a Spam Filters tab where you can configure rules to automatically discard or quarantine inbound emails before they create tickets. This is especially useful for keeping marketing emails and automated system notifications out of your ticket queue.

❓ PromptAre spam or junk emails creating noise in your queue today? If so, we can set up filters to catch those before they become tickets.

Outbound Mailboxes

On the outbound side, you can configure unlimited outbound mailboxes — these are the addresses your team's notifications, ticket updates, and workflow emails send from. You'll designate one as the default, which is what goes out unless a workflow specifies otherwise.

❓ PromptWhat email address should system notifications and ticket updates send from? Is it a no-reply address, a team address, or something else?
▲ Platform ImpactIf outbound mailboxes aren't configured, notifications will either not send or send from an unbranded system address. Customers receiving ticket updates will see whatever address is configured here — make sure it matches what they'd expect to see from your company.
→ Connects ToInbound email connects directly to Ticketing — inboxes are how email-to-ticket works. Outbound mailboxes connect to Workflows — you can specify which mailbox sends each workflow notification. Both affect what your customers see when they receive communications from you.
9
Billing Configurations — High-Level Overview

The last area we want to touch in System Settings is Billing. We're not going deep on billing today — that's a dedicated topic in Call 3 — but there are a few foundational decisions we should capture now so we can make sure the platform is set up to match how your business actually bills.

Billing Cadence & Net Terms

❓ PromptFirst — how often do you bill your customers? Is it monthly, quarterly, upon job completion, or some combination?
❓ PromptAnd what are your standard net terms? Net 30, Net 15, due on receipt? Do different customer types get different terms?

These answers shape how we configure your billing profiles and invoice defaults later. It's helpful to have them documented now so we're not starting from scratch in Call 3.

Rev.io Payments

Rev.io has a built-in merchant processing option called Rev.io Payments. If you use it, your customers can pay invoices directly through the customer portal, and payments post automatically to the invoice — no manual reconciliation. It also supports ACH and card-on-file for recurring billing.

❓ PromptAre you familiar with Rev.io Payments, or do you already have a payment processor you're planning to stick with?
💡 Internal NoteRev.io Payments is the preferred and most tightly integrated merchant option. If the client is interested, they'll need to complete a merchant application — flag this as a follow-up action and connect them with the appropriate contact to start that process. Don't sell or oversell it in the call; just introduce it and gauge interest.

If They're Not Using Rev.io Payments

If you're using a different payment processor, Rev.io PSA still integrates with a number of third-party options. We'll cover payment processor setup as its own topic in Call 3 — for now, just make a note of what you're using so we have it ready.

❓ PromptIf you're not using Rev.io Payments — which processor are you planning to use? We'll make sure we cover the setup for that in Call 3.
→ Connects ToBilling configurations connect to Invoice Templates, Bill Profiles, Accounting Periods, and Payment Processors — all covered in Call 3. What we capture here is the foundation that Call 3 builds on.

Action Items

  • Document billing cadence and net terms for Call 3 reference
  • Note which payment processor client plans to use
  • Flag Rev.io Payments merchant application as follow-up if client is interested
